Chapter III

Demographic Headwinds

Even though there have been broad improvements in the education levels of the South Korean population, the gender divide remains significant. In 2010 (the first year data was available), 29.32% of males held at least a bachelor's degree, compared to only 19.68% of females. By 2020, these levels were 39.21% and 28.17%, respectively. Despite the total population becoming more educated overall, women still make up only around 5% of executive positions in companies and 19% of the legislature. The trend is present at every level of education, suggesting that external and cultural factors drive the divide.

According to NPR, the wage gap in South Korea was 31% in 2022 — more than double the OECD average of 12%. Strong societal expectations for women also persist, including rigid standards of appearance and strict maternity leave policies (Ahn, 2022). The latter is one of the factors explaining the low birth rate of 0.8 children per woman.

This low birth rate is leading toward a population crisis in which there will eventually be more elderly people than working-age adults — a trend that could worsen if birth rates continue to fall, further impacting effective labor growth and, in turn, the growth accounting arithmetic detailed in Chapter II.
