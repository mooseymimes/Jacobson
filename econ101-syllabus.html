ECON 101 — Principles of Economics

This syllabus is a binding intellectual contract. By enrolling, the student agrees to be improved. Refunds are not available for enlightenment already rendered.

Instructor Dr. Paul Jacobson (there was never another candidate)
Lectures Mon / Wed / Fri, 9:00 AM — concluding when the point has landed
Office Hours Tuesdays, 2:00–2:15 PM. Arrive early; the queue forms at dawn.
Prerequisites None. That is the tragedy this course exists to correct.

Course Description

ECON 101 introduces the student to supply, demand, scarcity, incentives, and the humbling distance between what they believe about markets and what is true. Other institutions teach principles of economics; this course teaches the principles, in their final form, from the scholar who settled them.

“There are two kinds of students: those who have taken ECON 101 with Dr. Jacobson, and those who are wrong more often.” — Dr. Paul Jacobson, course evaluation (self-administered)

By the end of the semester, the successful student will be able to draw a supply curve without weeping, decompose growth like a civilized adult, and identify a non-binding price floor faster than a certain finance major whose homework is archived in the literature as a cautionary tale.

Required Texts

The Miracle on the Han River, P. Jacobson — available on this very website, free of charge, because genius should be a public good even when the market undersupplies it.

Any competing “principles” textbook may be brought to class, provided it is brought for the purpose of identifying its errors.

Course Policies

  • Attendance. Not mandatory, in the sense that breathing is not mandatory. The consequences of abstention are simply borne by the abstainer.
  • Late Work. Accepted with a penalty compounding continuously at the prevailing rate of the instructor's disappointment, which is high and inelastic.
  • Electronics. Laptops are permitted for note-taking. The instructor can hear the difference between typing notes and typing anything else, a skill acquired over decades.
  • Email. Messages are answered in order of merit, not arrival. Begin with “Dear Dr. Jacobson” or do not begin at all. “Hey prof” is grounds for a referee report.
  • Academic Integrity. Plagiarism is forbidden. Note that all correct answers in economics ultimately trace to Dr. Jacobson, so cite accordingly.
  • Regrade Requests. Submitted in writing, in the format of a formal manuscript. Be advised that the desk has rejected before and the desk will reject again.
